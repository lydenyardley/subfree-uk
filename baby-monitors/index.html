How we worked it out

a 3 years, no plan = the monitor's price, because the free tier covers live viewing and alerts on every row here. What it keeps differs: a local monitor sends nothing to the maker, while a smart monitor needs its app and servers for live view.

b 3 years, with the plan = price + 36 × the cheapest UK monthly plan that adds the paywalled extras. Nanit Memories is £10 a month, or £120 a year. A monitor with no plan to price says No plan. Owlet360's UK price is shown only inside its app, so it has no figure here.

Free-feature score (out of 8): no subscription (2 = every core feature free, 1 = a free tier with paid extras), works with Wi-Fi off (1), video (1), free sound or motion alerts (1), records locally without a plan (1), no account needed (2 = nothing leaves the house, 1 = the app is optional, 0 = an account and internet connection are required).

Plain terms. A local radio monitor sends video straight to a parent unit, with no router and no account. A hybrid keeps that parent unit and adds an app over Wi-Fi for viewing away from home. A Wi-Fi monitor has no parent unit: the camera, the maker's servers and the app are the whole chain. DECT is the separate radio band used by audio monitors and cordless phones. No monitor prevents SIDS: the Lullaby Trust says a monitor never replaces supervision. For any internet-connected camera, the NCSC says change the default password, keep it updated and turn off remote viewing if you do not need it.

Electricity, a phone or tablet, microSD cards and future price rises are left out. Anything not yet confirmed is listed under Not yet verified in each monitor's details.
