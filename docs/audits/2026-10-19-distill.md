# Impeccable distill: SubFree UK

- Target: `index.html`, the 14-row UK video doorbell comparison
- Run: 19 October 2026, read-only
- Mode: Read plus Operate
- Constraint: this pass removed nothing. It records the cuts and the evidence
  so a later edit can be one deliberate change, not a guess. Four of them have
  shipped since; the removed complexity log at the end holds them.

## The one job

A reader arrives with a budget and a worry: does this doorbell need a
subscription, and will it work in the UK. The page's one job is to let them
choose one of 14 models with the trade-offs visible, then leave.

The 20% that delivers 80% of that: the verdict, the six key numbers, the
recording column, the price row, and the compare tray. Everything else is
support, and support should be one tap away.

## Where the complexity is

Measured on the generated page:

| Surface | 390px | 1280px | Why it feels heavy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whole page | 8,720px | 2,971px | Five to six phone screens before the footer. |
| Table | 5,935px | 1,672px | 14 rows, six CSS labels each on phones. |
| Method block | 2,207px | 720px | 52 flat footnotes plus method prose. |
| Phone labels | 84 | 0 | Six repeated `data-label` labels per row. |
| Compare, Reolink trio | 18 body rows | same | Four rows are identical for that selection. |
| Controls | behind Filters | 8 visible | Two show-only, three power, two ranges, one sort. |

These are the 19 October figures. The sources disclosure and the phone label
column have since taken the method block and the table down; the removed
complexity log records what the page measures now.

The page is not visually noisy. It is long, and it repeats labels and proof
that the reader only needs on demand.

## Essence, kept

- The verdict and its recorded assumption.
- The six key numbers.
- The recording state and the three-year cost.
- The compare tray and its difference row.
- Details disclosures: price, voice, power, notes, sources.

These are the product. A cut that touches them is not a distill, it is a
different page.

## Numbered suggestions

1. **Collapse identical spec rows in compare into one counted line.** A
   "Differences only" control now hides them and names the count, which covers
   the reading problem. What is still missing is the other half: a single line
   in place of the hidden rows, so the reader can see that three rows were
   dropped and not wonder what went. Never collapse the decision rows (records
   free, price, the three-year cost) even when they match, because equality
   there is the finding.

2. **Remove the sort-time bar stagger.** Every sort sets 14 bars to zero, then
   runs 14 timeouts to draw them back. The bars are `aria-hidden` decoration.
   Draw once when the table first enters view, or paint instantly, and delete
   the timers. Lost: the ripple on re-sort, which nobody needs twice. (The
   forced reflow this item also named is gone; see the removed complexity log.)

Optional, lower confidence:

3. **Shorten the "Best for" lines on the rows.** Several run 12 to 16 words.
   Six words plus the verdict tag would scan faster. Do not shorten the
   verdict's pick reasons; they are arguments, not labels.

4. **Move the Local / Home Assistant filter behind a secondary disclosure on
   desktop.** It is a second axis on a 14-row page and the same fact sits in
   Details. This takes the visible controls from 8 to 7, which is not a
   transformative win. Test whether anyone uses it before spending the click.

## Verification plan for any accepted cut

- Rebuild, then re-measure page height at 390 and 1280.
- Re-run the audit; every assertion about controls, labels or counts must be
  updated in the same change, not weakened.
- Check the empty state still names a real model after removing a control.
- Check the keyboard path and the compare cap still work.
- Confirm no source number is orphaned when the list is regrouped.
- For the compare collapse, confirm the decision rows never collapse.

## Removed complexity log

Four cuts shipped on 20 October 2026, and the decisions behind them are in
`docs/decisions/0002-labels-sources-and-compare.md`.

- **Sources, grouped and folded.** The 52 links now sit in a disclosure,
  grouped under the site each points at, closed below 821px and open above
  it. Every footnote number is unchanged because each `li` carries its number
  as a value. At 390px the method block fell from 2,207px to 964px and the
  page from 8,720px to 6,834px. Not deleted: one tap restores the full list,
  and print opens it. The expectation here was no height change at desktop,
  and that part missed: 24 host headings make the open list 1,004px at 1280px
  against 720px for the flat two-column list. Grouping by brand instead would
  have hit the "six groups" shape, but shared sources such as the seven Home
  Assistant pages would then need duplicate entries, and an id cannot be in
  two places without breaking its footnote number.
- **The price label.** Gone from the phone card, because the pound sign
  already names the field. The other four spec labels moved into one muted
  column with the value beside them, which took the table at 390px from
  5,935px to 5,186px. The catch keeps its label above its prose, which a
  136px column would have squeezed.
- **The closing copy.** One line now carries the date, the promise about
  affiliate links and tracking, a way to report a mistake and a way back to
  the table. The method's duplicate invitation and the photo-processing
  sentence went with it, which took the method block another 19px down.
- **The sort's forced reflow.** `void tbl.offsetWidth` left the bar draw. The
  task boundary already commits the zeroed bars; measured, they still start at
  zero and animate to the score. A sort fell from 50 to 90ms of script to 5 to
  16ms. The stagger stays, because drawing once is still on the list above.

## What not to cut

- The recorded assumption sentence. It is the page's character.
- The difference row in compare. It is the decision.
- The Details disclosures. They are the progressive disclosure that lets the
  table stay dense without hiding facts.
- The empty state's named model. Recovery is part of the product.
- The five phone labels that name a number or a catch. They prevent guessing.
