# Delight audit: SubFree UK

**Target:** [index.html](../../index.html) · 14-row UK video doorbell comparison
**Run:** 19 October 2026 · after the CSS pass in this change
**Mode:** Read + Operate — a research surface that ends in a purchase decision, used once or twice, often on a phone while standing in a hallway.

---

## The delight thesis

> **The reader should feel they have just read the best-argued page on the subject, and that the page did the arithmetic for them.**

This is not a place for whimsy. The emotional range is dry, evidenced and quietly opinionated — the tone of a good consumer guide, not a brand campaign. Delight here means **relief**: the relief of a decision made cheap, of an assumption stated rather than buried, of a dead end that turns into a nudge.

That constrains where personality belongs:

- **At the short answer**, where the reader arrives with a vague question and leaves with a named model.
- **At the comparison**, where the specs become a decision and the money should do the talking.
- **At recovery**, when a filter asks for something the dataset cannot give.
- **At completion**, when the reader has a shortlist worth sending to someone.

Everything else — sorting, filtering, the table — should be quiet and immediate. Reliability carries the page; personality visits four times.

---

## What already lands

These are not findings to fix. They are the register the rest should match, and they should be protected in any redesign.

**D1 · The verdict is an argument, not a summary.** Three named picks, each with a reason and a consequence ("Blink and Arlo need extra hardware"). The 3px colour rules are the page's strongest visual signature: marginalia in a printed guide. It is the first thing that makes the page feel authored.

**D2 · The tray speaks like a person.** "1 selected (tick 1 or 2 more)" is a small piece of interface writing that anticipates the next move instead of reporting state. Most compare trays say "1 item selected" and stop.

**D3 · The "Only with extra kit" marker is a half-filled square.** The score bar, the filled/half/empty record markers and the `n/8` figure form one small, coherent notation. The page never explains it with an icon legend, and it doesn't need to — the shapes read at a glance.

**D4 · The assumption is confessed in the sentence that uses it.** "…a microSD card or USB stick, assumed at £10.00; that last figure isn't from a cited source." That candour is the emotional core of the page's trust. It is also the driest joke on the site, and it works.

**D5 · The page is fast because it is honest.** 106 KB of HTML, 55 KB of WebP, no webfonts, no analytics, no third-party requests. On a phone at a front door, it appears instantly. That is a delight you feel before you read a word; do not spend it on a font or a map.

## Findings and opportunities

**Status on 19 October 2026:** D7 (nearest match), the minimum copy from D12, and D14 (favicon and link card) shipped in the implementation pass. D8 was declined, so the price labels stay as bounds. The sections keep the audit's original wording; each shipped item is marked inline.

### D6 · The comparison stops at specs when the decision is money

**Moment:** The reader has ticked two or three rows and pressed Compare. The table shows 17 spec rows: records free, price, score, 3-year costs, catch, power, storage, Wi-Fi.
**What is missing:** The one row that resolves the decision — the difference in three-year cost — is present as two numbers the reader must subtract. The cheapest column is not marked.
**Why it belongs here:** This is the page's thesis ("what each one costs over three years") arriving at its payoff. Doing the subtraction is exactly the work the page promised to do.
**What to build:** A first body row, "3 years, cheapest", that names the cheapest selected model and prints each column's delta: `£195.63 · cheapest` / `+£64.00` / `+£158.00`. Use the existing tabular numerals and one muted colour; no badges, no confetti.
**Effort / impact:** Low / high. It uses data already in `D`, adds one row to `ROWS`, and no new dependency.
**Guardrail:** Mark the cheapest with text, not colour alone. If a model has no plan-based figure (`—`), print `no plan` and exclude it from the comparison rather than treating it as £0.

### D7 · The empty state can nudge toward the nearest match

**Status:** shipped. The empty state names the closest model and the price it needs, and one button widens the handle to include it. A conflict between two filters still ends at Reset.
**Moment:** The reader has dragged the price window below the cheapest model, or combined "PoE" with a price cap that excludes every PoE model.
**What used to happen:** "Nothing matches those filters. Reset filters." The only way forward is backwards.
**Why it belongs here:** This is the one moment the page can feel stupid. It knows the whole dataset; it can see the nearest match. Turning "no" into "here is the closest yes" is the most useful personality the page could have.
**What to build:** When `n === 0`, compute the nearest visible-if-relaxed row (smallest absolute price distance, then the cheapest relaxed match) and offer: "The closest match is the Tapo TD21 at £44.99 — show it?" The button widens the relevant handle by the minimum step and applies. Keep Reset as the secondary action.
**Effort / impact:** Medium / high. It is ~15 lines in `apply()` and the copy is the whole feature.
**Guardrail:** Never silently change the reader's filters. The nudge is a button they choose, and the count line must say what changed after they press it.

### D8 · The price window doesn't speak

**Status:** declined. The labels read £40 and £300 at the bounds; the bounds are accurate and the reader is not misled.
**Moment:** First paint. The two outputs read "£40" and "£300" — the dataset bounds, presented as if the reader had chosen them.
**Why it belongs here:** The first state of a filter should read as "everything", not as a constraint. The current labels make the page look pre-filtered.
**What to build:** "No minimum" and "No maximum" when a handle sits at its bound; then "£40–£300 · 14 of 14" in the count line once either handle moves. On the phone, the same words fit: the outputs are on their own line.
**Effort / impact:** Low / medium. One conditional in `apply()` and two `textContent` writes.
**Guardrail:** Keep the accessible names "Lowest price" / "Highest price"; only the visible value changes. Do not remove the £ figures when they are real values.

### D9 · The jump-to-row flash is visual only

**Moment:** The reader presses "Reolink Video Doorbell PoE" in the short answer. The row scrolls into view, flashes amber for 1.8 seconds, and focus lands on its checkbox.
**Why it belongs here:** The flash is the page's one authored motion, and it is genuinely helpful — it answers "where did I go?". But a screen-reader user gets the focus move and no explanation, and a reader with reduced motion gets a static tint.
**What to build:** A visually hidden polite live region that says "Reolink Video Doorbell PoE, best overall, row 2 of 14" when the jump completes. Reuse the same region as the filter and selection announcements; one `role="status"` for the page is enough.
**Effort / impact:** Low / high for the people who need it. It costs about 20 bytes in the build and no visual change.
**Guardrail:** Announce once per jump, not on every re-render. Clear the region after ~4 seconds so a second jump re-announces.

### D10 · The theme change is a switch, not a moment

**Moment:** Pressing "Dark mode" in the masthead.
**What happens now:** The page swaps instantly and the label flips. The half-disc icon never moves.
**Why it belongs here:** The icon is already a tiny diagram of the change — half light, half dark. Making it turn is the one place a physical gesture belongs, and it takes 180ms.
**What to build:** Rotate the disc 180° and cross-fade the page background and text colour over 180ms. `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` removes the rotation and keeps the instant swap.
**Effort / impact:** Low / low-medium. It is the smallest possible signature, and it makes the one global control feel considered.
**Guardrail:** Do not animate the whole table's colours row by row; that is expensive and fussy. Do not delay the swap behind the animation.

### D11 · The score bars are the page's only chart and they never move

**Moment:** The table paints, then the reader sorts by "Free-feature score".
**What happens now:** The 52px bars are static and identical in weight, so the sort produces no visual story.
**Why it belongs here:** A bar that draws once, from zero to its value, turns a column of numbers into a comparison the eye can make without reading. Redrawing after a sort makes the reorder legible.
**What to build:** A 200ms width transition, staggered 20ms per visible row, run once on first paint and once per sort change. Respect reduced motion by rendering the final width immediately.
**Effort / impact:** Low-medium / medium. One transition and one class toggle; no library.
**Guardrail:** Never animate on every `input` event from the price slider — that would make dragging feel laggy. Animate on sort and on first paint only.

### D12 · Three is a scold, not a rule with a reason

**Status:** the count-line copy shipped ("maximum, untick one to swap"). Replacement plus Undo remains open.
**Moment:** Ticking a fourth compare box.
**What happens now:** Every other box goes disabled. Nothing says why.
**Why it belongs here:** The cap is right — four columns stop being comparable on a laptop — but the interface treats the reader as if they should have known.
**What to build:** Make the fourth tick replace the oldest selection, and let the tray say "Swapped out Ring — Undo" for five seconds. If replacement is too much, at minimum change the count line to "Three at a time keeps it readable — untick one to swap." That sentence is the delight: it explains the constraint in the page's voice.
**Effort / impact:** Medium (undo) / high; low (copy) / medium.
**Guardrail:** Undo must restore the exact previous selection, not just re-add the row. Do not auto-scroll or steal focus when a swap happens.

### D13 · A shortlist worth sending is trapped on the page

**Moment:** The reader has two models selected and wants a second opinion from a partner or a forum.
**What happens now:** They retype the names and prices, or screenshot the compare table.
**Why it belongs here:** The page's promise is to make the decision cheap; the decision rarely happens alone. A plain-text shortlist is the most natural artifact the page could hand over.
**What to build:** A "Copy shortlist" button in the compare head. It writes name, price, 3-year cost and main catch for each selected model, plus the page URL and "checked 18 October 2026", to the clipboard. No image generation, no share SDK.
**Effort / impact:** Low / medium. `navigator.clipboard.writeText` with a plain-text fallback; one button.
**Guardrail:** Confirm with a short "Copied" label change, not a toast. Do not add a third-party share widget; the page's no-external-request property is part of the product.

### D14 · The first brand moment is a blank tab and a bare link

**Status:** shipped. The page has a local SVG favicon, a canonical URL, and Open Graph title, description, image and alt.
**Moment:** Opening the page in a tab; sending the URL in a message.
**What used to happen:** The favicon 404ed and the link unfurled as a bare URL with no title card.
**Why it belongs here:** The page's identity is strong once it loads; before it loads it has none. This is the cheapest place to be memorable.
**What to build:** A 32px SVG favicon using the existing paper/green palette (a doorbell outline or the split disc), and Open Graph tags with one of the local photos. Keep both in the repo; no CDN.
**Effort / impact:** Low / medium. Two files/tags, no runtime cost.
**Guardrail:** The OG image must be a real product photo already licensed on the page, not a generated graphic.

### D15 · The quiet moments are correctly quiet — leave them alone

**Not every interaction needs a flourish.** The filters apply instantly, the Details rows open without animation, the lightbox appears with the native dialog, and the table sorts without easing. That restraint is what makes D6–D14 land. Do not add:

- a loading spinner (there is nothing to load),
- confetti or sound on compare,
- a cookie banner (there are no cookies),
- a scroll-triggered fade on every row,
- a new font, analytics script or share SDK.

The page's byte budget is the delight: 161 KB total, zero external requests. Every idea above can be built inside 5 KB and no new dependency.

## Sequencing

If only one thing is built: **D6** (the compare-money row) — it is the page's own thesis, paid off at the exact moment of decision.

If two: **D6 + D13** (copy shortlist). Together they make the page feel like it is thinking with the reader, in the two places where it currently stops. D7 is already shipped.

If the goal is accessibility as delight: **D9 + the existing filter/selection live region** — the same 20 bytes serve both.

If the goal is signature motion: **D10**, then **D11**. Both are one transition and one reduced-motion guard.

---

## Verification contract

Every item above is only done when:

1. It works with the keyboard alone, in the same order a sighted reader would use it.
2. Any state change it announces is announced once, politely, and is also visible as text.
3. `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` removes the motion without removing the information.
4. It adds no external request and no dependency, and the page stays under ~200 KB total.
5. `python build/check.py` still exits 0, with axe clean in both themes at 1280/820/390 and no horizontal overflow at seven widths.
