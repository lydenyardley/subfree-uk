The short answer PitPat is the best without a subscription, at £149. PAWVA X01+ is the cheapest GPS tracker, at £78. AirTag is the cheap cat option if your cat stays near phones. Tractive and the other GPS trackers need a plan; yearly is cheaper than monthly.

The short answer

Best without a subscription: PitPat, £149. Its data is included for the life of the tracker, and live updates arrive about every 10 seconds.

Cheapest GPS: PAWVA X01+, £78, with no monthly fee. PAWVA's pages disagree on the SIM term, five years or ten.

Best for cats: Tractive CAT 6 Mini if you will pay, or an AirTag, £29, if your cat stays near other people's phones.

Avoid if you won't pay monthly: Tractive DOG 6 does nothing without a plan, and monthly billing is the dearest way to keep it running.

How we worked it out

a 3 years, no plan = the tracker's price, plus any collar or holder it needs. A tracker whose core function needs a plan is still shown at its price alone, and the cell says it does not track without one.

b 3 years, with plan = price + 36 × the cheapest UK monthly plan that keeps the tracker working. Paying yearly is cheaper: Tractive's Basic year is £84, Pawfit's £71.88, and Weenect's yearly rate is £6.67 a month. A tracker with no plan says No plan. Every tracker's sums are under Details.

Free-feature score (out of 8): no subscription (2 = tracking works with none, 1 = a Bluetooth tag rather than GPS, 0 = a plan is required), true GPS (1), live updates at a usable interval (1), location history free (1), safe-zone alerts free (1), cat fit (2 = made for cats, 1 = works on a cat with care, 0 = dogs only).

Plain terms. A GPS tracker works out its position from satellites and sends it over a mobile network, so it needs a SIM and signal. A Bluetooth tag has no GPS and no SIM: it is only found when a nearby phone on the Find My network hears it. A safe zone is a boundary you draw in the app, which alerts you when the pet leaves it. Live tracking is the fast update mode; the normal interval saves battery. UK 3G is switched off and 2G will go by 2033, so a 2G fallback is weaker than it sounds (Ofcom).

A collar, a phone's data when roaming, batteries, charging and future price rises are left out. Anything not yet confirmed is listed under Not yet verified in each tracker's details.
